Waste Navigation Guide

Learn how to properly identify, dispose of, and recycle different types of waste materials for a healthier environment.

Plastic Waste
Common Items:

Bottles, containers, packaging, bags, wraps, toys, cutlery

Environmental Impact:

Non-biodegradable, persists for hundreds of years, pollutes oceans and harms marine life, microplastics enter food chain

Proper Disposal:

Clean and sort by type (look for recycling numbers 1-7). Place in blue recycling bins. Soft plastics may require special collection points.

Paper Waste
Common Items:

Newspapers, magazines, office paper, cardboard, packaging, books, notebooks

Environmental Impact:

Contributes to deforestation when not recycled. Biodegradable but produces methane in landfills

Proper Disposal:

Keep dry and clean. Flatten cardboard boxes. Avoid recycling paper with food contamination, wax coating, or adhesives.

Organic Waste
Common Items:

Food scraps, fruit/vegetable peels, coffee grounds, eggshells, yard waste, leaves, plant trimmings

Environmental Impact:

Produces methane (powerful greenhouse gas) in landfills when not properly composted. When composted, returns nutrients to soil

Proper Disposal:

Compost in home bins or municipal green bins. Avoid composting meat, dairy, oils which attract pests.

Glass Waste
Common Items:

Bottles, jars, containers, drinking glasses, mirrors, window glass

Environmental Impact:

Non-biodegradable but 100% recyclable without quality loss. Saves energy when recycled compared to making new glass

Proper Disposal:

Rinse containers. Remove lids and caps. Sort by color where required. Broken glass needs special handling.

Metallic Waste
Common Items:

Aluminum cans, steel cans, foil, metal bottle caps, metal containers, small appliances

Environmental Impact:

Mining for metals damages landscapes. Recycling saves 95% energy compared to new production. Metals don't degrade in quality when recycled

Proper Disposal:

Rinse food containers. Crush cans to save space. Large metal items may need special collection or scrap yard drop-off.

Blue Bin

E-Waste
Common Items:

Computers, phones, TVs, printers, batteries, chargers, electronic devices, appliances

Environmental Impact:

Contains toxic materials (lead, mercury, cadmium) that can leach into soil and water. Valuable metals can be recovered through proper recycling

Proper Disposal:

Never throw in regular trash. Take to designated e-waste collection centers, retailer take-back programs, or community collection events.

Hazardous Waste
Common Items:

Paint, chemicals, pesticides, cleaning products, motor oil, fluorescent bulbs, aerosol cans

Environmental Impact:

Toxic to human health, wildlife, and ecosystems. Can contaminate soil, water, and air if improperly disposed

Proper Disposal:

Take to hazardous waste collection facilities. Never pour down drains or place in regular trash. Many cities have special collection days.

Textile Waste
Common Items:

Clothing, shoes, bedding, towels, curtains, carpets, fabric scraps

Environmental Impact:

Synthetic textiles can take hundreds of years to decompose. Production requires significant water and resources. Dyes can leach harmful chemicals

Proper Disposal:

Donate usable items. Recycle through textile collection programs. Repurpose as rags. Many retailers offer take-back programs.

Plastic Waste Management
Types of Plastic Waste
  • PET (1): Water bottles, soda bottles
  • HDPE (2): Milk jugs, detergent bottles
  • PVC (3): Pipes, shower curtains
  • LDPE (4): Grocery bags, shrink wrap
  • PP (5): Yogurt containers, medicine bottles
  • PS (6): Styrofoam, disposable utensils
  • Other (7): Mixed plastics
Environmental & Health Impacts
  • Takes 400-1000 years to decompose
  • Microplastics contaminate water, soil, and food
  • Harms wildlife through ingestion and entanglement
  • Some plastics leach harmful chemicals (BPA, phthalates)
  • Production creates greenhouse gas emissions
Proper Disposal Methods
  1. Rinse containers to remove food residue
  2. Remove labels and caps when required
  3. Flatten to save space
  4. Check local guidelines for accepted types
  5. Take plastic bags to store collection points
Reduction Tips
  • Use reusable water bottles and shopping bags
  • Buy products with minimal packaging
  • Choose glass, metal, or paper alternatives
  • Avoid single-use plastic items
  • Buy in bulk to reduce packaging

Paper Waste Management
Types of Paper Waste
  • Office Paper: Printouts, documents, letters
  • Cardboard: Boxes, packaging materials
  • Newspaper: Dailies, magazines, flyers
  • Mixed Paper: Envelopes, note paper
  • Books: Hardcover, paperback, textbooks
  • Specialized Paper: Glossy magazines, catalogs
Environmental & Health Impacts
  • Trees harvested for paper production contribute to deforestation
  • Paper production consumes large amounts of water and energy
  • Chemical processes in paper production can pollute waterways
  • Biodegradable but produces methane in landfills when not recycled
  • Recycling paper saves 17 trees per ton compared to virgin production
Proper Disposal Methods
  1. Keep paper dry and clean
  2. Remove plastic windows from envelopes
  3. Flatten cardboard boxes to save space
  4. Remove non-paper materials (staples okay in most areas)
  5. Separate by type if required locally
Reduction Tips
  • Go digital for bills, statements, and documents
  • Use both sides of paper when printing
  • Opt out of junk mail and catalogs
  • Reuse packaging materials
  • Choose recycled paper products
